## Course Description
Visual analytics is the use of interactive visual interfaces to facilitate analytical
reasoning. It builds on the idea that humans and computers working alone are each
insufficient for today's data challenges, and that effective combinations of human
judgment and computational methods are needed to build human-in-the-loop systems.

Visual analytics bridges human-centered disciplines such as visualization and
human-computer interaction with computation-centered disciplines such as machine learning,
probabilistic methods, and knowledge discovery. The course covers both theoretical
foundations and practical application of integrated visual analysis techniques to
real-world problems.

## Course Objectives
After completing this course, students will be able to:
1. Explain human aspects of analysis — perception, cognition, sensemaking, critical
   thinking, and the analytical process
2. Explain computational aspects — data management, data transformations, knowledge
   representation, probabilistic methods, and text analytics
3. Synthesize ideas from visualization, human-computer interaction, machine learning, and
   knowledge discovery to help people understand data
4. Use visual analytics tools to analyze datasets from domains such as public safety,
   health, news, business, sports, and science
5. Recognize and critique misleading uses of statistics, visualization, and maps

## Prerequisites
INFM603, INST630, or JOUR652; or permission of the instructor. Restricted to students with
permission of the College of Information Studies.

**No programming or statistics background is assumed.** The three qualifying courses differ
sharply in technical content — INST630 (Programming Human-Centered Interfaces) and JOUR652
(Interactive Design and Development) both teach coding, while INFM603 (Information Technology
and Organizational Context) teaches neither programming nor statistics. Students arrive from
all three paths, so no shared technical starting point is assumed.

jeopardizes the quality of education and depreciates the genuine achievements of others.
All members of the University community share the responsibility to challenge and make

### Students with Disabilities
The University is legally obligated to provide appropriate accommodations for students with
disabilities. The Accessibility and Disability Service (ADS) works with students and
faculty to address a range of needs; see
[ADS accommodations](https://ads.umd.edu/accommodations). Students must have their
disability documented by ADS, which then prepares an accommodation letter for instructors.
Students are responsible for presenting this letter to their instructors.

### Extensions and Late Work
If you must miss a deadline, inform the instructor as soon as possible, indicating when you
will submit your work. This should be used only for extraordinary personal reasons
(personal illness, death in the family, and similar). The general policy is that late work
is deducted 10% of its total grade per calendar day, starting the day it is due. Accepting
late work and assigning late penalties is at the instructor's discretion.
